## What I think this system is

Primer is a static, browser-only tool with no build step and no server logic. It turns a teacher's lesson into a short practice "experience" for students:

- **Teacher side** (`author.html`). The teacher fills in a lesson form (objective, learners, student task, evidence of progress, misconceptions, constraints, material). The teacher then answers 5 interview questions one at a time, and each can be skipped. Primer then shows a generated "design": a character, success criteria, the questions the character asks, keyword "look-fors", a briefing, provenance, the "system instructions" and a history. The teacher can edit fields directly or type revision requests in a small command language. Each change makes a new version. The teacher then approves (for the classroom or for testing only) or rejects. After approval the teacher creates the "student experience", and can download it as a `.primer.json` file and view the sessions recorded on this device.
- **Student side** (`learn.html`). The student works through Prepare (briefing, highlighted key sentences, full material, two warm-up questions), then Practise (a chat with a scripted character for up to N replies), then Feedback (self-assessment first, then strengths, growth areas and "not assessed" items with quoted student words, then a transfer question).
- There is no language model. A rule-based engine matches the student's words against keyword lists. Everything is stored in localStorage, and sharing works by passing a file.

## Teacher path

The teacher entry point is Landing, then "I'm a teacher", then `author.html`, then "Create a new experience".

1. **Lesson form.** I invented "Why the Moon has phases" (Year 7, teachback, explain to a younger cousin), with 4 evidence lines, 2 misconceptions, 2 constraints and one pasted paragraph. Setting: "No 3D world (conversation only)". When I submitted the form empty, all the required-field errors appeared in a single paragraph ("A learning objective is required. A description of the learners is required. …"). None of them sits next to its field, and "Short title" is not required.
2. **Interview, "Question 1 of 5" to "5 of 5".** The questions cover the character, the stakes, how hard to push (Gentle / Moderate / Demanding), the help policy, and the transfer situation. I skipped the stakes question with "I'm not sure — let Primer decide". The flow was clear.
3. **Design review.** The design was generated straight away as "Draft · Version 1 of 1 · Teachback". I then:
   - clicked "Approve for classroom use" without a name, and was correctly blocked: "Enter your name in the box at the top of this page first."
   - changed the character's name with Edit (from "Priya Raman" to "Sam"). This created a new version.
   - sent revision requests: `stakes: …` (applied), `make it easier` (difficulty became "gentle"), and `Please use emoji in the questions`. The last one was not understood and was kept as a private note. Primer said so honestly: "Primer could not turn that into a specific change, so it was saved as a note for you only …".
   - The history showed 5 versions, each with an author and an action.
4. **Approval.** "Approve for classroom use" changed the status to "Educator-approved", with the message "Approved for classroom use. You can now create the student experience."
5. **Create the student experience.** The page then said "The student experience is ready." and offered "Open as a student", "Download experience file" (which downloaded `why-the-moon-has-phases.primer.json`) and "Student sessions on this device".
6. **Gating checks.**
   - Editing after approval created "Revised draft · Version 6 of 6", and students still received the approved v5: the character was still "Sam", not "Sammy".
   - A second, draft-only experience ("Draft only: fractions") did not appear on the student page. Opening it directly by id (`learn.html?exp=design-…`) gave "That experience is not available in this browser."
   - Rejecting that draft asked for a reason and then showed "Rejected. Edit or request a change to continue."
   - Opening the downloaded file through the student page's "Open an experience file" loaded the approved experience correctly.
7. **Teacher session view.** This shows the full numbered transcript, an "Evidence recorded" table, a "How the character adapted" table, and a summary of the feedback the student saw.

## Student path

The student entry point is Landing, then "I'm a student", then `learn.html`. My experience was listed under "From your teacher". The two samples were listed under "Sample experiences", labelled "Test build".

1. **Intro.** Shows the goal, the task, "Three parts … About 30 minutes at most", a first-name field, and Start.
2. **Prepare.** Shows the briefing, 5 highlighted key sentences (each tagged "Pasted material"), "Read all of the course material" (headed "PASTED MATERIAL"), and two warm-up questions.
3. **Practise with "Sam".** I sent 13 replies. They included "I dont know", a request for the answer, correct statements for all four criteria, a correction of a misconception, an off-topic line, and filler. The answer request was declined appropriately. However, after correct answers the character kept saying "I'm lost. Could you try again with one concrete example?", and it asked again about criteria it had already credited. Practice ended on the 13th reply with "Thank you. I think I could explain some of this to someone else now."
4. **Feedback.** Self-assessment came first. Then the feedback showed "What went well" with 4 criteria and quoted my words with "Our guess: this looks secure / developing". "Where to grow" said "No growth areas were identified". "Ideas to check" covered the misconception, followed by an answer-seeking note, an honest disclaimer ("produced by matching your words against your teacher's criteria, not by a human reading them"), and the transfer question. Finish led to "Experience complete".
5. **Sample "Salary negotiation: interests before positions".** It is labelled "Test build. Your teacher has not yet approved this for classroom use." I played 3 turns and then used "End practice now", which shows a native confirm(): "End practice now? Anything you have not been asked about yet will be marked as not assessed." The feedback correctly marked two criteria "Not assessed".
6. **Sample "Reading a limestone cave".** I opened it and read the intro and Prepare text only. I did not enter the 3D field site, as instructed, so the cave rendering and "Continue without it" were not exercised.

## Overall verdict

**Not ready for classroom use. Usable as a working prototype of the authoring and approval workflow.**

There are no blocking findings: startup works as documented and both end-to-end paths complete without errors. The teacher-authority side (C1, C7) is solid, and both the approval gating and the labelling are honest. The student-facing core is weak, and nothing on the screens warns the teacher about it before approval. The rule-based character asks keyword-salad questions and speaks incoherent first-person lines built from the teacher's description. It ignores correct answers. The feedback it produces credits the wrong quotes, or misses relevant work. These are five major findings (F1–F5) and they bear directly on C2, C4, C5 and C6. A teacher who approves a design after reading the review page would see the keyword-salad questions and the odd opening line. The teacher would not see the non-responsive behaviour or the misattributed feedback until after students had used it.
