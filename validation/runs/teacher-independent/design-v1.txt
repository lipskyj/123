Learning goal

Objective: Explain, in their own words, why an equilibrium system shifts after a disturbance by comparing the reaction quotient Q to the equilibrium constant K - and correctly say when K itself changes (only temperature) versus when only Q changes (concentration, pressure/volume), and that a catalyst changes neither. from you

Learners: First-year undergrads, about 120 per section, second semester of gen chem. Most can recite "the system shifts to relieve the stress" and get the direction right on multiple choice, but they cannot explain WHY in terms of Q vs K or reaction rates. Many are shaky on what "equilibrium" actually means. Mixed math confidence; roughly a third are English learners, so plain language helps. from you

Prior knowledge to activate: They can write an equilibrium expression and calculate K from equilibrium concentrations, and they know the "stress" version of Le Chatelier. They know exothermic vs endothermic and that catalysts lower activation energy. The preparation should refresh how to write Q from current concentrations and the idea that Q moves toward K. from you

Format: Teachback (the learner teaches a character) from you

Difficulty: supportive AI suggestion: check this
The coach offers a hint after 1 unsuccessful attempt(s) and moves on after 4. After 3 success(es) the bar rises and reasoning becomes required. An answer must be at least 6 words to count. Practice length: up to 12 learner turns.

Constraints: About 15-20 minutes, done as homework before recitation. Plain, friendly language - many students are English learners. Keep it qualitative: no ICE-table calculations, they get enough of those on the problem sets. Use the Haber process as the main example. from you

Scenario

Character: Jordan, a classmate in the same gen chem section who is cramming for tomorrow's exam with the learner. Jordan memorized "the system relieves the stress" and is confident but wrong about catalysts and about K changing when you add reactant. Jordan is friendly but skeptical: they will not accept a rule without a reason and keep asking "but why does it shift?" Jordan needs this for the exam and for the Haber process lab report. from you

Character's goal: this for the exam and for the Haber process lab report

Learner's task: Explain the idea to a confused classmate. The classmate is studying for the exam and keeps confidently saying the wrong things (like "the catalyst makes more ammonia" or "if you add N2, K gets bigger"). The learner has to correct them by explaining with Q vs K and rates, not just restating "it shifts to relieve the stress". The classmate should push back and ask "but why?" until the explanation actually holds up. from you

Stakes: Jordan is relying on your explanation and will use it right away. AI suggestion: check this
