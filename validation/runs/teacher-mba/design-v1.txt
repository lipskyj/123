Learning goal

Objective: Given a proposed project and a pile of numbers from the business, decide which cash flows are incremental to the accept/reject decision and defend each inclusion or exclusion with the "with vs. without the project" test — especially sunk costs, opportunity costs of assets the firm already owns, allocated overhead, and working capital. from you

Learners: Part-time evening MBA students in their second term, average about 7 years of work experience. Many are engineers, operations managers and product people; a few come from accounting. They've done the time-value-of-money and NPV mechanics sessions, so they can discount a cash flow. What they lack is judgment about WHICH numbers belong in the analysis — they tend to trust whatever the spreadsheet from the business unit gives them. from you

Prior knowledge to activate: They can compute NPV and IRR and know that cash flows, not accounting profit, are what get discounted. The prep should refresh: cash flow vs. net income (depreciation is non-cash but creates a tax shield), and the idea that a decision only cares about what differs between the alternatives. from you

Format: Roleplay (the learner faces a counterpart) from you

Difficulty: standard AI suggestion: check this
The coach offers a hint after 2 unsuccessful attempt(s) and moves on after 4. After 2 success(es) the bar rises and reasoning becomes required. An answer must be at least 8 words to count. Practice length: up to 15 learner turns.

Constraints: About 20–25 minutes, done before class as preparation for our live discussion. Professional, collegial tone — Priya is a colleague, not a villain. Don't make them build a full NPV spreadsheet or compute taxes in detail; the point is the include/exclude judgment, not arithmetic. from you

Scenario

Character: Quinn, priya Nair, the Dayton plant controller. She's experienced, numbers-savvy and proud of her cost list; she wants the line approved but also wants the market test "paid back" and the overhead charge included because head office insists. She's not hostile, but she's confident and will push back with accounting logic ("depreciation is a cost", "the warehouse is free, we own it"). She concedes when the learner gives a clear with-vs-without reason, not when they just cite a rule. from you

Character's goal: the line approved but also wants the market test "paid back" and the overhead charge included because head office insists

Learner's task: Act as the analyst advising the capital committee. Priya, the plant controller, pushes her cost list as-is; the learner has to go item by item, say include or exclude, give the with-vs-without reason, and hold their ground politely when she pushes back ("but we spent $180k on that test — it has to be recovered!"). from you
