# Halvorsen Foods: The Dayton Line Extension (mini case, MGMT 6210 Session 7)

Halvorsen Foods is deciding whether to add a refrigerated oat-milk line at its Dayton plant. Priya Nair, the plant controller, has sent the capital committee the following "project costs":

- **Market test, completed last spring: $180,000.** Already paid. Priya wants it "recovered" by the project.
- **Unused warehouse wing.** The line would occupy a wing Halvorsen already owns. Priya lists its cost as $0. A logistics firm has offered to lease that wing for $95,000 per year.
- **Corporate overhead allocation: $120,000 per year.** Head office charges every new line 8% of revenue. Total corporate overhead will not change if the line goes ahead.
- **New filling equipment: $1.4 million**, depreciated straight-line over 7 years.
- **Net working capital:** inventory and receivables will rise by $210,000 at launch and be recovered when the line is discontinued.
- **Cannibalization:** marketing estimates 15% of oat-milk sales will come from customers who would otherwise buy Halvorsen's existing almond-milk product.
- **Interest on the equipment loan: $84,000 per year.** Priya subtracts this from the operating cash flows.

Your task as the analyst: for each item, decide whether it belongs in the project's incremental cash flows, and say why using the question "What changes for Halvorsen if we accept versus reject this project?" Note that depreciation is not itself a cash flow, but its tax shield is. Financing costs are captured in the discount rate, not in the cash flows.
